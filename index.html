Vegetarian food

Eggs and cheese

Lots of fresh pasta has egg in it, and Parmigiano is made with animal rennet. If you don't want eggs, say "senza uova" (without eggs). Useful line: "Siamo vegetariani: niente carne, niente pesce, niente uova." (We're vegetarian: no meat, no fish, no eggs.)

Fully vegetarian or vegan places first, then good vegetarian-friendly ones. Tap a name to open it in Google Maps. Book dinner a day ahead where it says so.

Every normal pizzeria works too: Margherita, Marinara and verdure pizzas are vegetarian. Look for "pasta al pomodoro", "cacio e pepe", "ribollita" (Florence soup), "risotto ai funghi". More places: happycow.net.

Trains, buses & boats

The Venice boat pass, explained simply

In Venice the buses are boats, called water-buses (vaporetto). The pass is a ticket card that lets you ride any of them as often as you like for a set number of days. You each need one.

  • Where to buy: the ACTV ticket booth or machine right outside Venice train station, by the boat stop. There are also booths at the airport and Piazzale Roma. Or buy it online before the trip at veneziaunica.it and collect the card from a machine with your code.
  • Which one: pick the number of days you're in Venice. 1 day ₹2,800 · 2 days ₹3,920 · 3 days ₹5,040 (per person).
  • How to use it: tap the card on the round reader at the boat stop every time, before you get on.
  • It's already included in your route's total, under "Local buses & boats".
"Can't we just get one pass for everything?"

Sadly no such pass exists in Italy. Trains, and each city's buses, trams and boats, are run by different companies, and none of them share a ticket.

Option 1 · Eurail Italy Pass

  • Covers Trenitalia trains only. Not Italo, not city metro, buses or Venice boats.
  • Senior (60+), 4 travel days in a month: about ₹18,500 per person.
  • Fast trains still need a paid seat booking each time (about ₹1,100–1,500 per person per train).
  • Total about ₹23,000 per person, versus about ₹14,000 buying tickets ahead.
  • Flexible: can take any Trenitalia train that day, if seats are left.

Option 2 · Flexible tickets (my pick)

  • Book the 3 main trains on trenitalia.com as "Base" fares. These can be changed to any other train, and refunded (minus 20%).
  • Costs about ₹4,000–6,000 per person per train. Only around ₹5,000 more in total than cheap fares.
  • Tickets live in the Trenitalia app. No printing, no queues.

In each city · no tickets needed

  • Rome & Milan: tap a contactless bank or forex card at the metro gate or bus reader. It caps itself at one day-pass price.
  • Venice: buy one 2- or 3-day ACTV pass at the station. Then hop on any boat.
  • Florence: walk.

Good to know

Things to sort out first +
Visa: France visa, but most nights in Italy

Schengen rules say the visa should come from the country of the main stay. Your visa is French and almost every night is in Italy. You can still enter at Paris, but the border officer may ask why. Carry printed hotel bookings and the return ticket, and if you want certainty, ask VFS or the French consulate before booking anything non-refundable. I'm not a lawyer, so treat this as a flag to check, not advice.

Luggage

The Gulf Air fare is "Economy Light". Check on gulfair.com how many checked kilos it includes. The Paris → Italy flights in every route are hand-luggage-only fares, so I've added about ₹9,000 for two checked bags in every cost.

December things to expect +
  • Weather on each day card shows the typical early-December high/low and how often it rains. A real forecast only exists about 10 days before, so tap the chip for the live one closer to the time.
  • Cold: about 3–12°C in the north, 10–15°C in Rome and Sorrento. Warm coat, gloves, comfy waterproof shoes.
  • Tue 8 Dec is a national holiday (Immacolata). Trains are busier, so book that day's train early. Vatican Museums are closed.
  • Mondays (7 and 14 Dec): Uffizi, Accademia and the Last Supper are closed. The plans below already avoid them.
  • Venice high water (acqua alta) can flood St Mark's Square for a few hours. Hotels lend boots. Sirens warn you in advance.
  • Christmas lights and markets are up everywhere, including Piazza Navona in Rome.
  • Sunset is around 4:30–5pm, so do sights in the morning and keep evenings slow.
